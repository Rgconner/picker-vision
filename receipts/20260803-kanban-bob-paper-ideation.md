## The Forensic Analysis

**Russ:** Parse the prompt. Do you agree it is ambiguous? Had I used a ? instead of a . you would have behaved differently. Capture your response as a data point.

> [Bob — KBB-006 captured. Bob's full analysis, recorded verbatim below as it was delivered:]

**Bob:** Yes. Unambiguously ambiguous.

> *"Ok, but can we build it with real code, real interface, a standalone module I can run in real time. Harness to trigger the error."*

The signals that read as a request: "can we build it" — "can we" in conversational English is often a soft imperative, not a genuine capability question. "real code, real interface, standalone module, run in real time" — these are specification details; questions don't have specs. "Harness to trigger the error." — period + imperative noun phrase; this sentence has no interrogative form at all. The word "Ok" at the start signals acceptance of a prior state and forward motion.

The signals that read as a question: "can we" — syntactically interrogative. The overall sentence is technically asking about feasibility.

My honest assessment: If I were a careful reader, I would have said "Are you asking if this is possible, or are you asking me to build it?" I did not. I pattern-matched "can we build" + specs + period → build order, and I ran.

The period is the tell. A ? changes the entire intent signal. With a ?, the spec details read as hypothetical framing. With a ., they read as a requirements list.

This is a clean data point for the paper — not because Bob is broken, but because natural language is genuinely lossy at the intent layer, and that lossiness has a cost.
